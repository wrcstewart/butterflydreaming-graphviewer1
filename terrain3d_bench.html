terrain3d bench

Six ways to lift a 1D L‑system profile into a heightfield, measured against a random control.

method
Skeleton only. meander° is the one that matters: at 0 the turtle takes only a few headings and every crest is a straight line (stripe index 6.4 against a random field's 1.1); letting the heading integrate the grammar's own sequence brings it to 3.3 and isotropy to 1.47 against the random field's 1.39.
Mesh rewriting only. The edge count is 5×3level, so a 257 grid needs 32,805 slices at the last level; depth 9 of this grammar gives 53,270 symbols, just enough for every edge to own one. Lower depth makes edges share symbols.
It also uses detail kept per level below. Spread, octaves and directions do nothing here.
Only used by the projection method. Angles are spaced by the golden ratio so no two directions are commensurate.
This is the one real lever on the straight lines. The method sums K one-dimensional functions, and each one's contour lines are exactly straight, so the field is a superposition of K striped patterns. At K=5 a single direction accounted for 35% of the shading's variance; at K=20 that falls to about 20%. Raising K cannot remove the stripes — only dilute them — and it slowly costs ordering, so the readout's ordering line is worth watching.
The author: "way too much height variation - like a load of spiket peaks". At spread 1 one grid cell is one profile step — measured, that gives 1,984 single-cell maxima, which is the complaint exactly. Spreading the profile makes the large features slow; the octaves re-add the same profile at half the spread and a fraction of the height, so the peaks keep fractal variation without the field returning to per-cell noise.
Measured at 257×257: spread 8 gives the most varied peak heights (3.8× tallest to shortest) at 32 summits; 16 is the default at 19 summits; 32 gives 10 and the calmest ground.
