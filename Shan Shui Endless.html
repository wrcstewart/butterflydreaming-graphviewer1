Sun, moon & shadows

The sun crosses the sky from 6:00 to 18:00. Where the moon is depends on the day of the lunar month, below.

Minutes of the day that pass each second. 0 holds the light still; 10 is a whole day in 2.4 minutes.

Days since new moon (a lunar month is 29.5 days). 0: new moon, beside the sun and unseen. About 7: half lit, up in the afternoon and evening. About 15: full, rising as the sun sets. About 22: half lit the other way, up after midnight and in the morning. The lit side always faces the sun. Time flow also advances this, one day per 24 hours.

Degrees above the horizon at noon. Low gives long shadows all day, as in winter or the far north.

Rotates the whole path around you. At 0 the afternoon sun is behind you to the left; at 90 it sets straight ahead, behind the ridges.

Tilts the view above the ground you are looking at. Dragging alone cannot do this: the view always points at a spot on the ground ahead, so moving back only flattens the angle.

How much of the scene fits top to bottom, in degrees: a wide-angle lens at the high end. Wider shows more sky and makes distant mountains smaller.

Mountains cast shadows onto the ground on their far side from the light. Softness blurs the shadows' edges.

Natural changes colour only in the last 10° above the horizon, from near white to a warm cream at sunrise and sunset. A pale disc is faint against the paper by day.
