spectral terrain

Method B: amplitude ∝ |k|−(H+1), inverse FFT, real part. No construction order, so nothing for a streak to follow.

surface
H near 1 is smooth and rolling, H near 0.5 is rough and Brownian. The readout checks the H that comes OUT against the H asked for, from the spectral slope −(2H+2) — near unbiased here, within 0.016. The variogram slope 2H is shown too but reads systematically low on these fields, by 0.14 at H = 0.95, because an inverse FFT produces a periodic tile and the wrap flattens the large lags.
scale
A soft high-pass, amp × k⁴/(k⁴ + k₀⁴). Suppresses anything wider than this and leaves the fractal scaling below it, so one massif becomes a range. Defaulted to 64 cells rather than off, because off is a single hump.
Optional radial smoothstep that gathers the tile into one mountain group. Off by default: it is a window on the result, not part of the method, and it makes the edges unrepresentative of the statistics the readout measures. H is measured before it is applied, since it destroys the periodicity the spectral estimate relies on.
view
A fixed multiplier, not a rescale. The heights are drawn as the method produces them, so the relief genuinely changes with H and with the cutoff — raw relief spans 0.29 to 9.68 across the settings. The readout says what share of the frame it fills.
Elevation spectrum only. 0 is the quasi-continuous scale; above 0 quantises into countable contours with a metre range in the legend.
A low sun is the lever for shallow relief: shading sensitivity goes as grade × cot(elevation), so 12° shows a grade the overhead sun flattens. Default 12°.
measure
The anisotropy run costs a 2D FFT plus eight synthetic nulls, so at grid 512 it adds a second or so. Turn it off to drag the sliders freely.
Calibration measures four fields of known stripe angle plus one with no direction at all, and must pass before any direction label on this page is worth reading.
