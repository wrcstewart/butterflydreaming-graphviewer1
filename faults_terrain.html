Method A: fracture and lift. Each step is a random line with the ground raised on one side by Q·sgn(d)·|d|α. Thousands of scarps sum to an fBm surface with H = α + ½.

surface
α = H − ½. α = 0, i.e. H = 0.50, is four times faster because it skips a Math.pow per cell — and it is also the case where the relation is most exact, measured 0.498 ± 0.022.
More faults means higher ground, not a different H: relief grows as √faults while measured H is unchanged (0.769 at 2,000 against 0.764 at 8,000, over six seeds). Below about 500 the individual scarps are still visible as straight steps, which is worth seeing once.

Logarithmic, and still a fixed multiplier. Raw relief runs 112 to 2,777 across the settings because it accumulates with √faults, so a linear slider cannot reach both ends. Nothing is rescaled per field, so adding faults really does raise the ground.
A low sun is the lever for shallow relief: shading sensitivity goes as grade × cot(elevation). It also throws the scarps into relief, which is the thing to look at here.
