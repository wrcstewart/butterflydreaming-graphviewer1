# Grain sources — provenance

Every sample bundled with `bd_M_DroneFrac` is listed here. **A sample with no
entry does not ship**, because the module is published CC0 and a file whose
licence we cannot state is a file nobody downstream can use.

This matters more here than in the sampler modules. A granular drone's identity
IS its source file: the script names a sample and reproduces nothing without it.
So the provenance is not a courtesy, it is part of what makes a shared script
mean the same thing at both ends.

The list the module actually offers is `manifest.json`, regenerated by
`../make_manifest.py`. It carries no licence field on purpose: this table is the
record, and a file that cannot be stated here should not be in the directory.

## The two organ pads

Both are **derived works** built by `../make_organ_pads.sh`, which downloads the
notes and rebuilds the pads from scratch. That script is the provenance: a
derived work nobody can rebuild is a derived work whose licence cannot be
checked.

**Upstream.** [Versilian Community Sample Library](https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL),
**CC0-1.0**. Renaissance Organ, "Full" registration, room mics. The instrument
carries its own grant in `Aerophones/Edge-blown Aerophones/Pipe Organ/Info.txt`:

> Organ sampled by Simon Dalzell of Ivy Audio, Winter 2015-16.
> Bearer is granted right to redistribute this sample set by Versilian Studios LLC.
> Credit to both original author and Versilian Studios is encouraged.

Credit is encouraged rather than required, and is given here gladly.

**Why an organ.** Granular wants material with no transients and no decay, and
an organ sustains mechanically — wind through a pipe does not fade. The raw note
measures **0.2 dB RMS spread** across its sustain.

**Why a chord and not a note.** One organ note is a single static pitch:
granulating it gives texture but no journey. Several copies a few cents apart
BEAT against each other, and that supplies the slow internal movement a drone
needs. Each voice is detuned, so each loops at a slightly different rate and
their seams never coincide — there is no single loop point to hear.

**A constraint worth knowing:** the sampled pitches are C D E F# G# A#, a
whole-tone set, so **there is no perfect fifth anywhere in this organ**. Hence
an augmented triad for the bright pad — the only symmetrical triad available,
and therefore the only one that sounds the same at every major-third
transposition — and plain octaves for the deep one.

## Register, and the thing that actually controls it

Rebuilt twice on 2026-10-02. The first attempt dropped the nominal octave and
**sounded identical**, which turned out to be informative rather than a delivery
fault.

**The registration matters more than the octave.** Measured on one note, share
of energy at or below C2:

| registration | ≤C2 | centroid | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full** | 10.9% | 337 Hz | a MIXTURE — upper ranks built in |
| **8′** | **46.3%** | **203 Hz** | the fundamental-pitch rank |
| 4′ | 2.9% | 261 Hz | sounds an octave UP, as 4′ means |

"Full" keeps most of its energy in partials well above its nominal pitch, so
moving the fundamental moves little of what you hear. The pads are now built on
**8′**, two octaves below where they started, and the difference is plain:

| | before (Full) | after (8′) |
|---|---|---|
| ≤C2 | 0.7% | **22.8%** |
| above 740 Hz | 22.5% | **4.3%** |
| centroid | ~700 Hz | **216 Hz** |

`REG_DIR`/`REG_PRE` at the top of `../make_organ_pads.sh` switch the stop; set
them back to `Full` for a brighter organ.

**VCSL labels its files an octave low** — the file called `C1` sounds 65.4 Hz,
which is C2. The recipes name files; the pad names use SOUNDING pitch. Worth
knowing before anyone reads an off-by-an-octave into either.

Built from lower files rather than pitch-shifted down: VCSL samples every
octave, so the low register is really recorded — the pipe's own character at
that pitch instead of a stretched copy, and no shift artefacts for the
granulation to magnify.

| | `organ_aug_C` | `organ_pedal_C` | `organ_single_C2` |
|---|---|---|---|
| files | C1, E1 +5¢, G#1 −5¢, C2 +3¢ | C1, C1 +4¢, C1 −4¢, C2 +2¢ | C1 only |
| sounding | C2, E2, G#2, C3 | C2 (×3), C3 | C2 |
| character | bright, symmetrical | deep, neutral | the bare note |
| duration | 36 s | 36 s | 36 s |
| RMS spread | **0.5 dB** | **3.4 dB** (the beating) | **0.3 dB** |
| RMS | −15.7 dB | −16.9 dB | −17.6 dB |
| peak | −7.3 dB | −8.6 dB | −12.4 dB |
| energy ≤C2 | 22.8% | 18.6% | 41.4% |
| centroid | 216 Hz | 271 Hz | 247 Hz |
| gain in the script | 1.10 | 1.97 | 5.98 |

**The gains have been re-solved twice**, and will need it again after any change
of note or stop. The 8′ rank is recorded far quieter than Full: these three
needed **+5.4, +10.9 and +14.7 dB** respectively just to reach the loudness they
had before. Three different gain numbers produce one loudness — the figures are
derived from measurement, never carried over, and changing a voice invalidates
them.

`organ_single_C2` is the **control**: the same file the bright pad's root uses,
trimmed and levelled and nothing else — no second voice, no detuning, no
beating. A/B it against `organ_aug_C` and what you hear added is precisely what
the recipe contributes, which makes the chording a choice rather than an
assumption.

Its 0.1 dB spread is the steadiest of the three, and that is the point: the
pads' larger figures are not noise, they ARE the beating. A perfectly steady
drone is also a perfectly static one.

**It is matched to the pads by RMS, not by peak.** The three sit within 1.5 dB
of each other in loudness, which is what an A/B needs. Note what that costs in
peak: the single note tops out 3.4 dB lower, because four voices occasionally
align in phase and one note never can. Peak-matching would have made the single
note audibly quieter and turned a comparison of texture into a comparison of
level.

The peaks are deliberate. **Headroom is the point of the gain setting, not
loudness**: granular SUMS overlapping grains, so a source at full scale drives
the module's own `Limiter(-1)` continuously and squashes the very beating the
detuning exists to produce. A first build landed at 0.0 dBFS and had to be
redone. The two pads also need *different* gains — octaves share harmonics and
so sum far more coherently than an augmented triad, putting the pedal pad 3 dB
hotter at identical settings.

## Withdrawn 2026-10-02 — Sample Focus material

`cosmic_pad_Dmin_60bpm` and `pad__in_love_A_min _2` came from
[Sample Focus](https://samplefocus.com/license) and **have been removed**. Their
Standard Licence grants a royalty-free right to use a sound "as part of a new
creative work", but prohibits sharing or making it available "in a complete,
archived, downloadable, or readily extractable format", prohibits redistribution
as part of a sample pack, and is **non-transferable**.

Serving the file from BD is exactly that: it was verified publicly downloadable
at `graph.virtualfictions.uk/bd_M_DroneFrac/sources/...`, HTTP 200, full file,
through the live tunnel. Committing it to a public repo would have been worse.

**And BD publishes CC0, which cannot be claimed over someone else's work.** That
is the more serious half: a false licence statement invites downstream users to
redistribute in reliance on it.

Note the **baked output is fine** — a granulated, detuned, reverbed drone is
plausibly "a new creative work", which the licence expressly permits. It was
only ever the source file that could not travel. The files are kept outside the
checkout at `~/bd_private_samples/` for local use, which the licence allows.

## vox_pad_Dmin7 — the vocal pad

**Source:** [freesound.org/s/625157/](https://freesound.org/s/625157/) — "VOX Pad
chord Dmin7" by **voxlab**, **Creative Commons 0**. Verified on the sound's own
page rather than assumed, which is the habit the Sample Focus episode below
bought. 11.63 s, 96 kHz, 24-bit stereo.

The author's own description: a synth pad made by processing a male vocal
recording — "O-Vowel. Smoothed with Steinberg Halion grain engine, processed
with Acustica Audio Ultramarine and MTurboEQ, resampled (96kHz)". Itself an edit
of a vocal recording by **PerMagnusLindborg**. Credit to both is given here
gladly; CC0 requires none.

That chain is also the answer to a question worth recording: **commercial
designer tools do not contaminate a CC0 result.** voxlab put a vocal through
HALion, Acustica and MTurboEQ and released the output CC0, which is correct — a
tool that only *processes* adds no licensable content. The care is needed when
an app contributes its own AUDIO: convolution impulse responses, bundled loops,
factory wavetables, sampled oscillators. Those carry their own licence into your
file.

Built by `../make_sample_pads.py`:

| | |
|---|---|
| window | 2.75–5.25 s, the loud central section |
| envelope | slow swell DIVIDED OUT, 0.5 s smoothing |
| transpose | **−1200¢, an octave down** |
| voices | 0¢, +6¢, −6¢ of the same material |
| duration | 36 s · RMS −20.2 dB · peak −5.0 dB |
| RMS spread | 4.9 dB — the pad's own movement, kept |

**Transposed an octave down.** At its native pitch this source measured **0.6%**
of its energy below C2 with a centroid of **476 Hz** — mid-range beside the
organ pads' 182–213 Hz, which is a real difference in instrument rather than a
fault. An octave down takes it to **15.4% below C2, centroid 332 Hz**. Still
brighter than the organ, because it is a vocal chord with formants where the
organ is a single pipe tone. `transpose=0` in `../make_sample_pads.py` restores
voxlab's original pitch.

Resampling down also doubles the loop from 2.5 s to 5 s, which is a free
improvement: fewer seams per minute.

**Why the envelope is flattened rather than just cut.** The file is a pad with a
long swell and a long decay: it rises from −38 dB to about −11 dB at 3.75 s and
falls to −43 dB. The flattest 2 s window still carries 4.4 dB of tilt. Loop a
tilted segment and the tilt becomes a pulse — you hear the loop breathing, which
is worse than the click it replaces. So a moving RMS over 0.5 s is divided out:
long enough to remove the swell, far too slow to touch the texture, which is the
thing worth having. This source is **already a chord**, so unlike the organ pads
no pitches are stacked on top — the extra voices are detuned copies, there to
beat and to stagger the loop seams.

**It is 3 dB quieter than the organ pads on purpose.** Its crest factor is near
14 dB against the organ's 5–10, so matching loudness left it peaking at −0.4 dB
with nothing spare. Granular sums overlapping grains, so headroom is matched in
preference to loudness.

## j8_pad_Amin9 — the Jupiter-8 pad

**Source:** [freesound.org/s/624728/](https://freesound.org/s/624728/) — "J8 /
Jupiter 8 - Citylights PD - Amin9 chord" by **voxlab**, **Creative Commons 0**,
verified on the sound's page. 20.25 s, 48 kHz, 24-bit stereo. The author's
description: three layers of TAL J8 (Jupiter 8) combined, processed and sampled
into an Amin9 patch with a 1990s aesthetic.

**The best-behaved source here by a distance.** Measured across its whole
20.25 s it sits between −11.9 and −14.2 dB — a 2.3 dB range, with no swell and
no decay. So unlike the vocal pad there was no window to hunt for and almost no
drift to divide out: a long smoothing (2 s) is used precisely so the patch's own
slow movement survives, that movement being its character.

| | |
|---|---|
| window | 1.00–17.00 s — sixteen seconds, no hunting needed |
| smoothing | 2.00 s (gentle; there is little drift to remove) |
| transpose | **−2400¢, two octaves down** |
| voices | 0¢, +6¢, −6¢ |
| result | 32.1% below C2 · centroid **205 Hz** · RMS −20.6 dB · peak −6.7 dB |
| RMS spread | 11.0 dB — the patch's chorus, slowed fourfold |

**Two octaves, not one.** At −1200 it still measured only **0.1%** of its energy
below C2 with a centroid of 394 Hz — brighter than the vocal pad, because an
Amin9 voicing from three Jupiter-8 layers sits high. −2400 brings it to 205 Hz,
inside the organ pads' 183–217 Hz. It also slows the patch's own chorus movement
fourfold, which suits a drone, and stretches the 16 s window into a **64 s
loop** — longer than the finished 36 s pad, so the voices never wrap and there
is no loop seam at all. The best answer to a seam is not to have one.

**It is 3.6 dB quieter than the organ pads**, for the same reason the vocal pad
is: an 11 dB swing puts its peaks far above its average, so matching loudness
would have left it peaking at −3.7 dB with nothing spare for grain summing.
Headroom is matched in preference to loudness throughout this directory.

## aura_pad_Amin — and a provenance caveat

**Source:** [freesound.org/s/697998/](https://freesound.org/s/697998/) — "ATM
Aura Amin (135BPM)", **Creative Commons 0** per Freesound's own page. 28.44 s,
44.1 kHz, 24-bit stereo.

**THE CAVEAT, and it is deliberately not buried.** The uploading account has
been **deleted**, and the description calls the sound part of a *"royalty free
ambient sound collection from SerenityRecords"*. Both of those weaken the
provenance relative to the voxlab uploads, which are the author's own work:

- **"Royalty free" is not "redistributable."** That exact phrase is what the
  Sample Focus episode below turned on. If SerenityRecords released this as
  royalty-free and the uploader relabelled it CC0, the CC0 is not theirs to
  give.
- **A deleted account cannot be asked.**

Freesound's record says CC0 and that is what we can cite, so it ships — but it
is the **lowest-confidence entry here**, and if the module is ever published
this is the file to reconsider first. The voxlab and VCSL entries are sound.

**"135BPM" is only a tempo label.** Checked for transients before anything else:
**0 of 1422** windows above 3x the median. It is a texture, not a rhythm, so it
granulates safely.

| | |
|---|---|
| window | 13.50–15.50 s — the flattest part of a long swell |
| transpose | −2400¢ |
| result | 17.7% below C2 · centroid **211 Hz** · RMS −19.7 dB · peak −4.7 dB |
| loop | **8 s**, so it recurs 4.5 times in the pad |

**Two s and two octaves were chosen together**, and that is the point. At −1200
it measured only 2.9% below C2 (centroid 398 Hz), so it needed −2400 like the
J8. But transposing down lengthens the loop fourfold, and a 4 s window would
have become a 16 s loop — long enough to lose the repetition. Halving the window
gives an 8 s loop at the lower pitch: **low and recognisable**, which the two
previous pads each managed only half of.

## gtr_pad_Cmaj7 — the freeze-pedal guitar chord

**Source:** [freesound.org/s/870087/](https://freesound.org/s/870087/) —
"Sustained G-C maj 7 gtr chord" by **LAPS-Catalog**, **Creative Commons 0**,
verified on the sound's page. 9.17 s, 48 kHz, **Float32** stereo.

**The best-behaved source in the set.** A guitar chord held with a *freeze
pedal*, which is exactly why it works: the pedal sustains the chord
indefinitely, so there is no pick attack and no decay. Measured, **0 of 458
windows** above 3× the median, and flat to **2.8 dB across all 9.17 s**. A
plain guitar chord would have been the worst possible granular source — one
loud transient per loop — and the pedal is what makes it the best.

| | |
|---|---|
| window | 2.85–4.85 s |
| transpose | −2400¢ |
| result | **51.4% below C2** · centroid **173 Hz** · RMS −20.1 dB · peak −5.7 dB |
| loop | 8 s, so it recurs 4.5 times in the pad |

**The lowest and darkest of the seven**, and by some margin: over half its
energy lies at or below C2, against 28% for the organ pads. Two octaves were
needed despite it already reading 18.9% below C2 at −1200 — a guitar chord
carries a great deal of upper harmonic, so its energy sits higher than that
percentage suggests, and its centroid at −1200 was 326 Hz against 205–211 for
the other synth pads.

**It is also the file that broke the builder.** Float32 WAV cannot be read by
Python's `wave` module (format 3), and nor can WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE (0xFFFE),
which is what ffmpeg emits for any depth above 16 bits — so converting to
24-bit first does not help either. `read_any()` now decodes to **raw 32-bit PCM
on a pipe** and skips headers entirely, with ffprobe supplying rate and channel
count. FLAC, AIFF, 16-bit, float and mp3 all work now without a parser each.

## alchemy_gtr_Cmaj7 — a derived work, and the licence reasoning

The author imported **Freesound 870087** (CC0, LAPS-Catalog) into **Logic's
Alchemy** on its **Spectral** engine and bounced 78 s of a single held note:
24-bit/48 kHz, unnormalised, flat to 2.5 dB across the whole file, **0 of 3899
windows transient**.

**The licence position.** CC0 means the author waived everything, so the
material can be processed in any tool and the result licensed freely — the
constraint is only that **the tool must contribute processing, not content**.
Alchemy resynthesising *your own imported file* adds nothing licensable;
Alchemy's **factory presets and sources** would, being built on Apple's sample
library. The same line separates ChromaVerb (algorithmic, fine) from Space
Designer (convolution — its presets are recordings of real spaces).

**CONFIRMED 2026-10-03.** The chain was Alchemy on its **Default preset** with
the CC0 file imported into **Source A** on the Spectral engine, modulation off.

Default is not a contribution of content: an initialisation patch supplies
*settings* — filter type, envelope times, which effect slots exist — and
parameter values are not copyrightable audio. `cutoff = 12000` is a number, not
a recording. Alchemy's Default loads no sources, so everything audible in the
bounce came from the imported file.

The two things that would have broken it, and did not apply: a **factory
source** in slot B, C or D (Apple's sample library), or a **convolution**
reverb such as Space Designer, whose presets are recordings of real spaces.
Logic's algorithmic effects — ChromaVerb, Ensemble, Modulation Delay, EQ,
filters — contribute processing only and are safe in this chain.

| | |
|---|---|
| window | 20.00–22.50 s |
| transpose | **0 — none** |
| result | centroid **220 Hz** · 2.1% below C2 · RMS −16.6 dB · peak −4.5 dB |
| loop | 2.5 s, so it recurs ~14 times in the pad |

**No transposition, unlike every other Freesound pad here.** Alchemy's spectral
resynthesis had already dropped the centroid from the original's **553 Hz to
204 Hz** — a factor of 2.7, more than the two octaves the other pads needed.
Transposing again would have landed it near 51 Hz. Measured before building,
which is the only reason it was not shifted into uselessness.

**It is low by centroid but has little deep bass**: 2.1% below C2 against 51.4%
for the untreated `gtr_pad_Cmaj7` built from the same source. Alchemy
concentrated the energy into the midrange rather than extending it downward, so
the two pads from one guitar chord are genuinely different instruments.

**Did the Logic treatment add movement?** Frame-to-frame spectral flux inside a
2 s window went from **0.14** in the original to **0.41** in the bounce — three
times the change, and level with the vox pad's 0.45. So yes, measurably.

**But the metrics cannot tell interesting from dull, and this is worth
recording.** The vox pad (which the author found interesting) and the J8 pad
(which he found dull) measure **0.449 and 0.449** — identical flux — and their
centroid swings differ by only 4 points. Every number available here measures
*suitability*; none of them measures *interest*. Only listening does.

Three pads now come from **one guitar chord**, differing only in treatment:

| pad | treatment | ≤C2 | centroid | flux in 2 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gtr_pad_Cmaj7` | none | 51.4% | 173 Hz | 0.136 |

## Loop length is a musical choice, not a technical one

The author noticed that the vox pad, cut from a mere 2.5 s, was "more
interesting musically" than the J8 cut from 16 s — the opposite of what the
measurements predicted, the J8 being much the steadier source.

**The reason is repetition.** A 5 s loop recurs seven times in a 36 s pad, and
each time the three detuned voices stand in a slightly different relation to one
another: theme and variation, which the ear follows. The J8's 64 s loop is
longer than the pad itself, so it is heard once and never recurs — nothing to
recognise, so nothing develops.

So the seam-free long loop, which took three rounds to arrive at, is musically
the **weaker** option. **Steadiness is a measure of suitability, not of
interest.** `win` is the knob that trades them: roughly 2–5 s to be
recognisable, longer to be ambient and shapeless.

| pad | loop | recurrences in 36 s |
|---|---|---|
| organ (all three) | 4.3 s | 8.4 |
| vox_pad_Dmin7 | 5.0 s | 7.2 |
| aura_pad_Amin | 8.0 s | 4.5 |
| gtr_pad_Cmaj7 | 8.0 s | 4.5 |
| j8_pad_Amin9 | 64 s | **0.6** |

## The loop click, and how it was actually fixed

Reported as "higher frequencies come in briefly every few seconds and make a
slight popping noise". Diagnosed, then mis-fixed, then fixed:

| | step at the wrap (of 32768) | |
|---|---|---|
| hard `-stream_loop` splice | 998 | −30 dBFS — audible click |
| a 0.6 s self-overlap crossfade | 392 | better by 8 dB, still audible — **and removed, see below** |
| **5 ms fades to zero at both ends** | **8–225** | at or below the file's own typical sample step |

**The crossfade was removed because it broke the level.** Only the 0.6 s overlap
window had both voices present, so each pad came out loud for 0.6 s and 16 dB
quieter for the remaining 3.7 s, over and over — a far worse fault than the
click. It went unnoticed because the seam was measured and the ENVELOPE was not.
**A fix aimed at one property must be checked against the others.**

Two lessons worth keeping.

**Staggering seams does not remove them.** An earlier version of the build
script claimed that because each voice loops at its own rate "there is no single
seam to hear". The opposite: four voices give four clicks at different times
instead of one.

**A crossfade relies on luck; fading to zero does not.** Whether the two ends of
a loop meet at compatible points in the waveform cycle is chance, so a crossfade
only reduces the step. Both ends at silence cannot discontinue. The price is a
5 ms notch every 4.3 s in one of four voices — 0.1% duty on a quarter of the
energy — against a click 21 dB below RMS and broadband.

**And measure the right thing.** High-band energy *relative to the median* made
the genuine 8 dB improvement read as a 15 dB regression, because smoothing the
signal moved the median too. The sample-level step at the wrap is the honest
metric.

## What to check on a candidate before adding it

1. **Redistribution, in writing, with a URL.** Not "royalty-free" — that phrase
   is about *royalties*, and says nothing about whether you may pass the file
   on. Ask specifically whether the SAMPLE may be redistributed, not whether
   music made from it may be.
2. **CC0 or public domain only.** CC-BY cannot go into a CC0 repository: you
   cannot relicense away someone else's attribution requirement.
3. **Steadiness.** Window the RMS and look at the spread. Under ~4 dB is good;
   over ~10 dB and you are granulating events, not texture.
4. **Headroom.** Aim for a peak around −4 to −6 dB. Grains sum.
5. **Sustained, not plucked.** No attack at the front.
6. **Decodable by Web Audio** — MP3 or AAC. AIFF and FLAC need converting.
