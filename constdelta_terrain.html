constant delta terrain

The triangle scan with the same delta at every cell, in metres. Six controls, no grammar.

Derived, not guessed: for 1000 m of relief over the diagonal this has to be 1000 / 2(N−1), which is 1.95 m at grid 257 — so 2 m. The readout gives the far-corner height and the gradient for whatever you set.
The simplest structured sequence there is: +delta for N cells, then −delta for N cells, repeating. A step towards simulating the fractal without its complications — it is exactly balanced, so there is no drift, and the only thing in it is one length scale. Whatever the scan does with structure should show here in its plainest form.
Unticked gives the constant delta, where the surface is a ramp and the self-tests below have closed-form answers.
Only the cell size changes with this. The gradient for a given total rise does not: at 1000 m over 20 km it is 2.50% along an axis at every grid size, because the rise and the distance are what fix it.

It is back, and the self-test below asserts the two orders agree bit for bit under a constant delta.
Multiplies each cell's delta by its own lag, so every cell gains delta per diagonal whatever stencil it has. Measured, that makes the anisotropy worse: gain per unit of true distance goes from axis 1.000 / diagonal 1.010 to axis 1.000 / diagonal 1.414 — exactly √2, because a diagonal step covers √2 of distance and an axis step covers 1, so levelling per k over-rewards the diagonal. Left here only to show what per-k levelling does; the diagonal weight is the real fix.
At 1 this is true scale and the readout says so, but it also makes the delta slider look dead: over 20 km the whole plausible range of deltas is 2–8% of the frame at a grazing angle, which is real change and invisible change. Defaulted to 8 so the delta slider can be seen to work; drop it to 1 for the honest slope. The readout always gives the on-screen relief as a share of the frame, which is the figure that explains a slider that looks inert.
For shallow relief the ELEVATION is the lever: Lambert shading shifts a slope's brightness by about grade × cot(elevation) relative to flat ground, so a 1% slope moves brightness 1.0% at 45°, 1.9% at 28° and 4.7% at 12°. It was fixed at 28°, which is why the diagonal feature was faint.
The diagonal ridge runs along u = v, which is world (1,0,1). 135° and 315° light ACROSS it; 45° and 225° light along it and show nothing. The readout gives the sensitivity and warns when the azimuth is in the blind direction.
Only used by the elevation spectrum. Defaulted to 0, continuous — the author: "Bands are not much use please have a quasi continous colour scale". The ramp has 256 entries, so at 0 it is continuous for all practical purposes. Raise it only if you want countable contours with a stated metre range; the legend follows either way.
