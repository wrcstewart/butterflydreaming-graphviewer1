constant delta terrain

The triangle scan with the same delta at every cell, in metres. Six controls, no grammar.

Derived, not guessed: for 1000 m of relief over the diagonal this has to be 1000 / 2(N−1), which is 1.95 m at grid 257 — so 2 m. The readout gives the far-corner height and the gradient for whatever you set.
Only the cell size changes with this. The gradient for a given total rise does not: at 1000 m over 20 km it is 2.50% along an axis at every grid size, because the rise and the distance are what fix it.
The author: "although serpentine option shouldnt matter we need to test it" — right, and I had deleted it on the ground that it provably cannot matter, which is the hypothesis a test exists to check. It is back, and the self-test below asserts the two orders agree bit for bit under a constant delta.
The valley is not a bug in the code — it is what this weighting does, verified against the closed form. (u−1,v−1) is two diagonals back, so interior cells lag the axes and sit 20.7% lower. Ticking this multiplies each cell's delta by its own lag, which levels it exactly.
At 1 this is true scale and the readout says so, but it also makes the delta slider look dead: over 20 km the whole plausible range of deltas is 2–8% of the frame at a grazing angle, which is real change and invisible change. Defaulted to 8 so the delta slider can be seen to work; drop it to 1 for the honest slope. The readout always gives the on-screen relief as a share of the frame, which is the figure that explains a slider that looks inert.
For shallow relief the ELEVATION is the lever: Lambert shading shifts a slope's brightness by about grade × cot(elevation) relative to flat ground, so a 1% slope moves brightness 1.0% at 45°, 1.9% at 28° and 4.7% at 12°. It was fixed at 28°, which is why the valley was faint.
The valley runs along u = v, which is world (1,0,1). 135° and 315° light ACROSS it; 45° and 225° light along it and show nothing. The readout gives the sensitivity and warns when the azimuth is in the blind direction.
