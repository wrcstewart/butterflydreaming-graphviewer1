trianglescan terrain

Anti-diagonal scan from the u,v origin. Each cell's base is a reciprocal-distance average of its three established neighbours; the fractal supplies dH.

fractal
+ up, - down, > gentle up, < gentle down, = level — the same tokens as the other benches. One symbol is consumed per cell in scan order, so a grid of N needs N² symbols; the readout says whether the derivation is long enough.
scan
MEASURED, this grammar is NOT balanced: at depth 9 it holds 6,764 + against 14,514 -, a mean of −0.1438 per symbol. The bias is in the rules — X is net +2.09 but Y is −1.09 and Z is −1.00 — and it worsens with depth because Z triples itself each generation: −0.032 at depth 6, −0.170 at depth 10.
Unbalanced, the partial sums grow with exponent 0.933 — linear DRIFT, not a random walk — which at 257 and the automatic dH is a one-way ramp some seventy times the texture. Uncheck it to see that for yourself; it is almost certainly why earlier benches were "gradually downhill" throughout.
The three-point average has unity gain, so every cell adds one more dH and the wander accumulates. The automatic scale sets dH so the accumulated wander over the diagonal comes out near full relief; at a fixed 0.03 it was about ninety times it, and the texture would have been invisible.
Coloured #0d2060, a dark blue chosen so it reads by LUMINANCE and not by hue — worst contrast against any of the four schemes is 5.0:1, on the sea. 50 cells covers the diagonals up to k=9, so it marks the origin corner and the first few scan lines.
(0,0), (0,1) and (1,0) are yellow. At spread 0 that is literally three vertices of 66,049 — 0.005% of the surface, and invisible; the spread widens the patch so the corner can actually be found. The blue 50 are unaffected.
Bright yellow alone cannot work here: measured against the four scheme surfaces its worst luminance contrast is 1.2:1, so it would be telling you apart by hue only. It therefore has a dark ring, which guarantees a luminance edge whatever it sits on.

Equalises the rise per diagonal, and it is a diagnostic rather than a fix: measured, it makes the per-distance anisotropy WORSE, taking the diagonal from 1.010 to 1.414 times the axis gain, because a diagonal step covers √2 of ground and an axis step covers 1. The diagonal weight — now √2, not 1/√2 — is the real fix. Off by default.
The author: "the frequency of the bumps in general is way to high we need more slowly changing." One symbol per cell makes the cell the finest scale there is, and measured along the anti-diagonal the bumps came out at 18–23 cells — eleven to fourteen across a 257 grid.
Holding each symbol for several cells scales the structure up, and because a held symbol is a constant dH over those cells it gives a smooth ramp rather than a step. Measured at 385 from stretch 1 to 32: roughness 0.466 → 0.353, relief 0.206 → 0.415. It also multiplies the sequence available by the same factor, which is what makes the start slider below worth having.
The spectral peak is not a reliable single number here — it jumps between bins as the sequence's own structure interacts with the smoothing — so judge it by roughness and relief, both in the readout.
An L-system's opening is its least characteristic part — the first symbols come straight from the axiom's own expansion — so starting deep in the derivation is worth being able to do.
There has to BE room: at depth 8 the derivation yields 17,475 drawing symbols against the 66,049 a 257 grid consumes, so it wrapped 3.8 times and an offset would only have rotated a repeat. Depth defaulted to 10, which yields 162,019 — 2.45 passes — and 11 gives 7.45. The readout says how many passes you have.
The streaking along +u+v was predicted from the stencil at the outset and measured at elongation 4.38. It is the SINGLE CAUSAL DIRECTION, and the proof is that entering from the opposite corner gives 0.23 — the same factor the other way, 1/4.3. Averaging four scans, each consuming the sequence afresh in its own traversal, cancels the bias: elongation 4.38 → 1.00.
It costs amplitude, since averaging four partly-correlated fields cancels signal too, so dH is multiplied by 2 — the measured roughness ratio. Roughness comes back to 1.032 of 1.036 and relief to 79%. No normalisation involved; the amplitude is a free parameter.
Unchecking it makes every diagonal run the same way, which puts consecutive symbols at opposite corners between lines — a seam down one edge. With the FRACTAL it matters; under a constant delta it provably cannot, and the self-test on constdelta_terrain.html asserts the two orders agree there bit for bit.
